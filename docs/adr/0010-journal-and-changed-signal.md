# Append-only journal over the JSON checkpoint, plus a Changed signal

At a 10-year workload (10 Entries a day, 36,530 Entries — measured locally: a 7.9 MB pretty-JSON store, ~9 ms to rewrite it, ~5.5 MB of entry JSON in every Snapshot) every mutation rewrote the whole store before being acknowledged, and the GUI (1 s), tray (5 s) and TUI (1 s) re-fetched full Snapshots while idle. Neither cost is fatal at that size today, but both grow linearly with history, so the design gets worse exactly for long-term users.

Mutations now append one JSON line — the diff between the store before and after, as Entry/Project upserts and removed ids (`Journal::between`) — to a sibling `store.json.journal` file, synced before the mutation is acknowledged. The existing pretty-JSON document stays as the checkpoint: loading reads the checkpoint and replays the journal over it, and once the journal passes 256 KiB a mutation rewrites the checkpoint (the old atomic save) and truncates the journal. Replay is idempotent, so a crash between checkpoint and truncation is harmless, and a torn final journal line is the one unacknowledged mutation, safe to drop. A pre-existing `store.json` with no journal loads exactly as before — that is the whole migration. The service also emits a `Changed` D-Bus signal after each persisted mutation; clients refresh on the signal and keep only a slow fallback poll (30 s GUI/TUI, 60 s tray) that also covers clock boundaries, a service that appears later, and services too old to signal.

## Considered Options

- **SQLite.** Rejected: row-level writes are exactly the incrementality wanted, but it replaces a human-readable, hand-repairable file with a binary database, adds a C build to every target, and forces a one-way schema migration of users' only copy of their history — a large, hard-to-reverse change to solve what is currently a 9 ms rewrite.
- **Keep whole-store rewrites and polling, justified by the measurements above.** Rejected as the *endpoint* (it is what the issue exists to leave behind): the numbers are comfortable at 10 years on a fast disk, but every term — bytes rewritten per tap, bytes per idle Snapshot, fsync cost on slow media — scales with total history, and the journal removes the factor instead of arguing it is small.
